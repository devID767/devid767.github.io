Context

A test task for Voodoo: build a playable ad of Mob Control that runs as a single HTML file in any browser, under five megabytes, in one week. Unity and C# on the authoring side, Luna Labs to transpile it to the web.

Playable ads fake their crowds with sprites — this one runs a real 3D crowd

The differentiator

Playable ads fake crowds with sprites because real ones do not fit the budget. I wanted actual 3D units, which meant proving they could run on the slowest device anyone would open this on — a 1 GB Amazon Fire 7.

60 vs 10FPS against one object per unit

The straightforward approach died on measurement: one game object per unit gave ten frames a second at eight hundred units. So the crowd stopped being objects.

Units render from a vertex animation texture with a genuinely baked walk cycle, merged into batches of two hundred, with a separate texture carrying per-unit state. Colour space moved from linear to gamma, which is what brought the Fire 7 back to life at all.

The result was sixty frames a second at eight hundred units and forty-seven at fifteen hundred, on the cheapest tablet in the room.

Enemies, gates, a tower and a fever bar had to fit the same week

Scope

Gameplay: a stream of enemies with mutual attrition on a grid, multiplier gates up to ten with a zone query and a guard against recursive re-entry, a tower with health, a damage zone, a wall, a blocker, a fever bar, and a champion unit with its own skinning and a fan of gate clones.

Presentation: a window system for menu, game, win and lose with end cards, transitions on DOTween, a two-light MatCap setup with a pastel palette and distance fog, and contact shadows — a blob under the cannon and a planar silhouette under the tower and gates through a single-pass per-caster shader with stencil and edge clipping, plus a shadow for the crowd drawn through the same batch as the crowd itself.

Effects and feel: wheel smoke, muzzle flash, tower explosion, idle glints on the gates, spawn and death animations driven through the state channel of the crowd texture, an audio system with a voice pool, music from the first tap, and a tutorial sequence rebuilt from the reference.

The transpiler had its own rules. Some maths functions do not survive it, so crowd rotation had to be re-encoded as a direction vector after a bug that existed only in the web build.

The last day was a performance session: shadows reinstated, champion pooling, culling of crowd batches, simulation gated while in the menu, and a warm-up pass on load. Combat holds thirty frames on the Fire 7.

Worth calling out

I built this one end to end. These are the parts worth naming.

The crowd
  • Vertex-animation-texture rendering with baked animation, merged batches and a per-unit state texture.
  • The measurement that killed the naive approach before it cost a week.
  • Batch culling, pooling and a load-time warm-up pass.
The game
  • Enemy flow and grid attrition, multiplier gates to ten, tower health, damage zone, wall, blocker, fever bar and champion.
  • Match loop across menu, play, win and lose, with end cards and the install call to action.
The look
  • Two-light MatCap setup, pastel palette and distance fog matched to the reference.
  • Contact shadows through a single-pass per-caster shader, including one for the crowd.
  • Effects, unit animations, audio with a voice pool, and the tutorial sequence.
